## 15. Portfolio Storytelling Direction

The portfolio should not feel like a gallery of finished designs.

Each important project should communicate:

**Problem → Thinking → Solution → Execution → Result**

The focus should be on **why the project existed, what problem Gading identified, how he approached it, what he created, and what changed as a result**.

For example, a web-app case study should not simply say:

> “Built a web application.”

Instead, explain:

> “An existing workflow was repetitive and inefficient. Gading identified the bottleneck, designed a simpler workflow, built a web-based solution, and turned the process into a faster and more structured system.”

Likewise, a campaign case study should show that Gading was involved in the **thinking and concept development**, not only the visual execution.

## 16. Tone for Portfolio Copywriting

The portfolio should feel:

* Modern
* Confident
* Clear
* Intelligent
* Creative
* Practical
* Human
* Professional without sounding corporate

Avoid exaggerated corporate phrases such as:

* “visionary leader”
* “industry-leading”
* “world-class”
* “high-impact solutions” when unsupported
* “end-to-end” when unnecessary
* “technology evangelist”
* “AI expert” unless specifically supported

Avoid making Gading sound like:

> “A graphic designer who also knows AI.”

Instead, communicate:

> **“A creative marketer who uses design, technology, and AI to solve problems and build better solutions.”**

The writing should be concise and easy to understand, with enough personality to feel like a real creative professional rather than a generic corporate profile.
